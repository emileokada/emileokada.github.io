Authors and mathematical sources

This atlas accompanies joint work of Dan Ciubotaru and Emile Okada on Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials for graded Hecke algebras and Arthur packets for exceptional groups. The geometric setting is a complex reductive group acting through the centralizer of a grading element on the degree-two part of its Lie algebra.

The polynomial computations follow Lusztig’s work on perverse sheaves on graded Lie algebras [1, 2] and its explicit formulation in Ciubotaru’s Multiplicity matrices for the affine graded Hecke algebra [3]. The present project develops these computations across the exceptional types and their generalized Springer series.

The microlocal approach to packets originates in Adams–Barbasch–Vogan [4] and Vogan [5]. Its p-adic formulation was developed by Cunningham–Fiori–Moussaoui–Mracek–Xu [6]. Packet membership is determined by the nonvanishing of microlocal vanishing cycles, equivalently by the occurrence of the relevant conormal component in a characteristic cycle.

Throughout the atlas, Arthur–ABV packets means packets defined by this microlocal construction. Comparisons with endoscopically defined Arthur packets are stated separately, with their hypotheses and references.

Coverage and conventions

The index lists classical types A₁–A₈, B₂–B₉, C₂–C₈, and D₄–D₈, together with exceptional types G₂, F₄, E₆, E₇, and E₈. It provides links to Kazhdan–Lusztig, Fourier-transform, and Arthur–ABV packet results for the neutral gradings listed.

The project’s intended exceptional-type coverage includes the Dynkin gradings attached to all nilpotent orbits, including the zero orbit and both even and non-even gradings, across principal and non-principal generalized Springer series. Completeness and series-by-series availability remain subject to confirmation against a release manifest.

When interpreting a dataset, the relevant conventions are:

  • The complex group, its isogeny type, and the weighted Dynkin diagram of the grading.
  • The centralizer of the grading element and its degree-two representation.
  • The orbit labels and the representations of component groups used to label local systems.
  • The generalized Springer series, the matrix row and column conventions, and the normalization of the polynomial variable.
  • The conventions for Fourier duality and packet membership.

Orbit numbers are local to a grading. An enhanced parameter consists of an orbit together with an irreducible equivariant local system. Cross-references between polynomial matrices, Fourier-transform tables, and packet tables use these enhanced parameters.

Release information and verification

A versioned release, publication date, code repository and commit, permanent data archive, and verification report are not yet specified here. Release metadata and download links will be added when available.

Planned release documentation and checks

The proposed archive will contain machine-readable tables, a manifest, reproduction instructions, and checksums. Datasets will be identified by group, grading, and generalized Springer series.

The verification report is intended to record, where applicable, diagonal normalization, compatibility with orbit closures, degree bounds, the defining matrix identities of the algorithm, Fourier involutivity, and consistency between characteristic-cycle data and packet membership. Comparisons with published tables and independently recomputed cases will be recorded separately.

Release history, archived versions, and a corrections contact will be added with the release information. An error report should identify the version, group, grading, series, and relevant parameter labels.

How to cite

Attribute tables and computations to Dan Ciubotaru and Emile Okada, Graded Kazhdan–Lusztig Atlas. A formal release citation and downloadable BibTeX entry will be added once the version and permanent identifier are available.

For the mathematical methods and results, also cite the relevant accompanying paper. To identify a calculation, specify the group, weighted Dynkin diagram or nilpotent-orbit label, generalized Springer series, and parameter labels. For a polynomial, give both the stalk and support parameters.
